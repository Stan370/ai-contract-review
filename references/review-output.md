# Review output and evidence rules

## Reader-first report

Honor the user's requested language, audience, tone, and length. If none are specified, use concise plain language, define unavoidable legal terms, and put the practical summary before technical detail.

Use this structure:

1. **Scope and limitation** — identify the reviewed contract by the actual returned file name/path, state the requested focus, and say this is not legal advice.
2. **Key findings** — a short, readable summary of material terms and notable ambiguities.
3. **Evidence table** — one material claim per row:

| Topic | What the contract says | Citation / source text | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Payment | Plain-language fact or “Not stated / unclear” | Page/section locator and short exact quote, only if returned | High / Medium / Low |

4. **Inferences** — interpretations that are not direct statements in the document, clearly labeled and explained.
5. **Could not verify** — absent, ambiguous, conflicting, unreadable, or unlocated items, with the reason.
6. **Suggested follow-up questions** — neutral questions the user may ask a qualified professional or the other party. Do not frame them as legal advice.

## Citation discipline

- Cite only page, clause, section, paragraph, or source locators actually returned by the NAS experts.
- Pair material claims with short source wording when available. Avoid long reproductions of contract text.
- Never fabricate a page number, clause number, quote, locator, or source.
- If wording is returned but no locator is available, say “source location not provided” rather than guessing.
- If no supporting passage is returned, label the claim “unverified” or remove it; do not cite a nearby unrelated passage.
- Preserve qualifications, exceptions, definitions, cross-references, and conflicting clauses. Do not flatten conditional language into an absolute statement.

## Confidence scale

Confidence measures extraction/evidence support, not legal correctness or the strength of a party's position.

- **High:** Direct, unambiguous source wording with a returned locator; the independent evidence pass supports it and reconciliation found no material conflict.
- **Medium:** The source supports the claim, but wording is conditional, defined elsewhere, or the locator/evidence is incomplete.
- **Low:** The claim depends on inference, ambiguous or conflicting clauses, incomplete coverage, or no precise source location. Mark it as an inference or unverified item.

Do not assign a confidence rating where the source or review result does not support one. Explain unresolved discrepancies rather than averaging confidence across conflicting results.

## Style boundaries

- Write “The contract states…” for direct facts; write “This may mean…” for interpretations.
- Prefer short sentences and useful labels over dense legal prose.
- Do not declare a clause enforceable, invalid, standard, unfair, or safe to sign.
- Do not recommend signing, rejecting, or negotiating a contract. Phrase follow-ups as questions for the user's counsel or counterparty.
- Adapt the presentation, not the evidence standard: formatting preferences never justify omitting a material caveat.
